## Formants
When a sound is played in a room/cavity, certain frequencies resonate more than others (see [[Room Acoustics]]). The audio spectrum will show certain harmonic peaks. These are called formants. 

We can apply this to the human mouth too. Our lungs cause a vibration in air, and then we can change the shape of the "room" by moving our tongue to produce different formants. This is what causes different vowel sounds when speaking, despite the fundamental frequency being the same.
## Vocoders
These look like a synth with a microphone on it. We need two signals:
- A carrier (e.g. a synth). This should be harmonically rich to get as many frequencies out of it.
- A modulator (e.g. a voice). This changes the properties of the carrier. 

These works by splitting the modulator into several bands, and then analysing each band for its amplitude. It then applies this amplitude to the carrier signal.
