## Shot 1: Phone 
Welcome to OddTime. Let's explore how to create and visualise your own polyrhythm in seconds.
## Shot 2: Close up of the grid
Using a grid is a useful way of visualising polyrhythms.

We have a 3 against 2 polyrhythm here, so we've drawn a grid which is 3 by 2. Our pink pulse lands on the start of every row, and the purple pulse lands on every third cell. Then they're played together , it's called a composite rhythm. Let's have a listen to what this sounds like.
## Shot 3: Close up of the controls
We can use the dropdowns here to change the number of beats each pulse has, let's say 3 and 4, and the grid will update accordingly. Let's hear what a 3 against 4 polyrhythm sounds like. We can speed it up if we want to.

We can switch around the pulses, to get the same polyrhythm, but a different downbeat. Here's a 4 against 3 polyrhythm.
## Shot 4: Settings
In the settings, you can customise the app how you see fit. Choose between light and dark mode, 4 different metronome sounds or master volume. Now, let's jam with a 3 against 5 polyrhythm.
