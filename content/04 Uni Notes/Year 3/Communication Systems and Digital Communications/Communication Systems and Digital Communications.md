[[Communication]] is the transfer of [[information]] from one entity to another. [[Information]] concerns change and can be quantified. Different types of signal contain [[information]] and are used to transfer information ([[transmission]]).

For example:
- *Radio waves transferring speech from a mobile phone to a base station*
- *A CD player reading stored information for audio playback*

## Key Steps
1. Creation of an [[information]] bearing message signal
	- *Audio, video, text...*
2. Creation of a signal for [[transmission]] 
	- *Electrical, electromagnetic wave, acoustic...*
3. [[Transmission]] through a physical medium (channel)
	- *Copper cable, optical fibre, air, free space*
4. Reception of the signal and extraction of information.

![[Introduction.png]]

## Types of Signal
A [[signal]] is a quantity that varies in time (and space)
- An analogue signal is a continuously carrying signal in both amplitude and time.
- A digital signal comprises a discrete set of amplitude levels which change at discrete times.

We are interested in [[signal|signals]] which contain [[information]] we want to transfer (message [[signal|signals|signals]]).
- Many are analogue, e.g. Speech & audio generated by humans
- Others are digital, e.g. Data stored on machines, sensor data

We are also interested in [[signal|signals]] to transfer [[information]] over the channel (transmitted [[signal|signals|signals]])
- Converting a message signal to a transmitted signal often involves [[modulation]] of a carrier [[signal]]. A few systems support direct transmission of a message.
- Many carrier signals and channels are analogue, e.g. EM waves correspond to continuously varying electric and magnetic fields in a continuum
- The modulation of a carrier signal with [[information]] can be analogue or digital. Almost all modern [[communication]] systems employ digital [[modulation]].
## Example
Let's say we have an analogue information source, like a speech signal, which continuously varies over time. Let's also decide we want to transmit this information source using analogue [[transmission]], over a radio frequency, by using amplitude [[modulation]]. 

![[Communications Introduction.png]]

1. Human speech is our input. It can't be transmitted over long distances.
2. It gets "translated"/[[modulation|modulated]] onto a radio frequency, where the shape of the input signal is "imprinted" onto the amplitude of the radio frequency. This is our passband transmitter/[[signal]]
3. The radio frequency travels through air, only around a specific range of frequencies. This prevents interference from other signals. 
4. The passband receiver demodulates/un-translates the specific frequency band of the desired signal, extracting the lower frequency speech signal from the higher frequency radio signal.
5. And we get our human speech out again.
## Analogue vs. Digital 

|                                     | **Digital**                                                                                      | **Analogue**                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Description**                     | Probably binary. Changes to discrete levels at discrete times.                                   | Can have any level and change at any time (often smoothly)                              |
| **Added Noise Effect**              | Gives *errors*. Hopefully not too many, which may be correctable.                                | Gives *noisy* output, not fully correctable.                                            |
| **Required transmission bandwidth** | *High* when directly representing analogue signals. Maybe *low* if exploiting signal redundancy. | As given (baseband bandwidth or similar)                                                |
| **Features**                        | If it works at all, then it should work properly                                                 | All component or system degradations cause overall output degradation, albeit graceful. |
Digital is super prevalent nowadays. Why? 
- It can resist corruption of the transmitted signal, as long as the noise is within limits. 
- It is possible to regenerate the signal with repeaters.
- Digital signals can be coded to detect and correct errors. 
- Use of digital hardware is very flexible, with microprocessors, reconfigurable FPGAs, etc. 
- It is easy to multiplex several signals together; the same link can be used to carry different types of messages. 
- Storage of digital signals is easy and inexpensive. 
## Communication Networks 
A [[communication system]] usually consists of many user terminals, connection via a network of links and nodes.

![[communication system.png]]

They are typically based upon a series of layers, to make the design process simpler. The International Standards Organisation reference model is used, called the Open Systems Interconnect (OSI). The Internet model is a good practical example. 

| **OSI Model** | **Internet Model** | **Description**                                                    |
| ------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Application   | Application/User   | Application/user interface: What services can the network provide? |
| Presentation  | Application/User   | "                                                                  |
| Session       | Application/User   | "                                                                  |
| Transport     | Transport          | End-to-end control: Can I talk to you? How?                        |
| Network       | Network            | Routing: Where are you? How do I get to you?                       |
| Data Link     | Link               | Link control: When can I transmit? Reliability?                    |
| Physical      | Physical           | Point-to-point [[transmission]]                                    |
